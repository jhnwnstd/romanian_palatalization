==============================================================================
HEADLINE — does the modern lexicon show Steriade's co-variation?
==============================================================================

  Question: on modern Romanian nouns with a target obstruent stem-final,
  do nouns whose plural takes a palatalising allomorph prefer
  the palatalising verbalizer -í over -á/-uí? (Steriade 2008 §10.2.3.5.)

  Design: 2×2 contingency, row = allomorph-stemwise (purely morphological,
  no per-noun mutation check), col = -í vs (-á ∪ -uí);
  Fisher exact + permutation null; Steriade's own exclusions applied.

  ANSWER (n = 452 noun-verbaliser pairs):
    pooled OR = 3.79  [95% CI 2.50, 5.76]
    Fisher exact p = 4.17e-10;  permutation p_perm = 0.0000 (zero of 10 000 shuffles beat it)
    null distribution:  median OR = 1.01,  97.5%-ile OR = 1.50

  Read as: the observed OR is far above what random reshuffling of the
  K/T͡ʃ-vs-K/K labels produces. Steriade's paper reported OR ≈ 50 on
  n = 157 hand-collected verbs; our OR is smaller because the modern DEX
  has more real K/K + -í nouns (gând-í, rost-í, vad-í …) than her sample.
  Direction and significance reproduce; magnitude is muted but real.

Loaded 501 (lemma, dvs, ...) tuples that pass row filters.
Etymology decisions: 715 explicit pairs, 4 user-keep lemmas, 374 verb verdicts.
