# Czech palatalization: data and analyses

Czech velar and r palatalization (kluk/kluci, ruka/ruce, sestra/sestře;
knížka, ruční, strašit) counted with the shared multilingual core
(`src/lexpipe/`), so that the Czech, Italian and harmonized Romanian
tables come from the same code. The question is the one of the project:
does palatalization in derivatives depend on the alternation a learner
hears in the base's inflection (Steriade 2008, inflection dependence),
or is it the default for underspecified roots, with resistance coming
from prespecified roots that select non-palatalizing allomorphs (the
underspecification account of Gorman & Winstead, `latex.tex`)?
Learnability is judged with the Tolerance Principle: a rule over N
items survives e exceptions when e ≤ θ = N/ln N.

- Plugin: `src/lexpipe/langs/czech/` (module docstrings are the
  reference for every definition below).
- Binding design: `docs/multilingual_design.md` (rules R1–R27).
- Every CSV: `docs/czech/data_dict.md`, generated by the `docs` stage
  from `src/lexpipe/langs/czech/schema.py`.
- Data-layer decisions and fixes: `docs/czech/data_layer_notes.md`.

```
python scripts/lang_pipeline.py --lang cs                    # every stage
python scripts/lang_pipeline.py --lang cs tp spellout        # some stages
python scripts/lang_pipeline.py --lang cs --force tp tp      # rerun one
```

Stages run in pipeline order and are skipped while their manifest
(`data/czech/manifests/`) matches inputs and parameters (R6). The seed
of every random step is the run context's (`--seed`, default 20080715).

On the full data the analysis stages take about 8 minutes (derivations
72 s, tp about 4.5 minutes, spellout 28 s, noevidence 19 s, covariation
69 s with 10,000 permutations per test, logtable 11 s, docs 29 s: it
validates every Czech CSV against the schema); the data layer 4 to 6
minutes. Every learner cell is evaluated on the same simulated
vocabularies (`lexpipe.learners.simulate_all`, run parameter
`workers`, default up to 8 forked processes); the output does not
depend on the number of workers. The MorfFlex projection is the
sub-stage `qc-morfflex` of `qc` (`--force qc-morfflex qc` rebuilds it).

The adversarial audit of 2026-10-23 led to the fixes listed under
"Data quality" below (stage versions: extract 2, lexicon 3, attest 4,
features 7, derivations 6, tp 7, spellout 4, noevidence 4, covariation
4, logtable 4, docs 3); every number in this file is from the run after

## Definitions

- **Lexemes and roots (R4).** A lexeme is an English Wiktionary entry
  (or a cs.wiktionary entry with no English counterpart); entries
  whose sense is a derivational form-of ("diminutive of kluk",
  "female equivalent of Čech", "verbal noun of kvasit") are lexemes
  too, since they have paradigms of their own (`derivational_formof`),
  while inflectional form-of senses, spelling variants and adjective
  degrees are not. `root_id` groups the lexemes of one word and
  etymology. TP counts root types; a root pools the evidence of its
  lexemes. Nouns and adjectives are separate records of a root. In the
  domain: 30,430 nouns and 13,852 adjectives (names, multiword terms,
  indeclinables, t-stems, irregular stems are out; the dual remnants
  ruka, oko and sto enter only as derivation bases, and ruka in the
  spell-out set).
- **Targets (design §6, `ortho.py`).** The stem final: velar k, g, h,
  ch (clusters sk, ck); r (r after a consonant, `Cr`, apart for the
  vocative); t, d, n (`coronal_inferred`: the change to ť ď ň is
  invisible in spelling and inferred before ě/i/í, so these rows are
  reported apart and have e = 0 by construction); c (only the -ec
  vocative otče); the **baseline** of hard-declension labial, l, s
  and z stems, whose front allomorphs involve no palatalization. Soft
  and i-stem declensions are `soft`, not targets. The stem is GEN.SG
  minus its ending (n/t/d before -ě is ň/ť/ď: ohně → ohň-, soft), the
  common prefix of the oblique cells its consistency check. Root types:
  8,295 velar, 1,612 r, 6,389 t/d/n and 5,222 labial, l, s and z nouns
  (of which the hard-declension ones form the baseline); 2,891 velar
  adjectives.
- **R26 suffix targets** (`morphology.py`) are identified
  morphologically: an etymology template or suffix category, a
  derivational form-of sense (klouček 'diminutive of kluk'), another
  lexeme's headword line (Čech f=Češka), every hard -ský/-cký adjective
  (-ický included: Czech has no simplex adjective root in -sk-/-ck-), or
  a base lexeme the word decomposes against when the word is neither
  inherited nor borrowed whole (sedlák ← sedlo; not banka, matka).
  Velar targets inside a suffix: 4,702 of the 8,342 in-domain velar
  noun lexemes and 2,698 of the 2,892 velar adjective lexemes (before
  the audit, with etymology and category evidence only: 1,807 of 7,671
  and 515 of 2,848).
- **Cells and allomorphs (§6).** P = the palatalizing allomorph, N its
  competitor:

  | cell | P | N (competing allomorph) | non-trigger front vowels |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | NOM.PL m.an | -i (adjectival nouns -í) | -ové, -é | |
  | LOC.SG m.in, n | -e/-ě | -u | |
  | DAT/LOC.SG f | -e/-ě | (none in the hard paradigm) | |
  | LOC.PL m, n | -ích | -ách | -ech |
  | VOC.SG m.an | -e after a velar, Cr, -ec | -u | -e of coronals and of Vr |
  | INS.SG m, n | | | -em |
  | adjective NOM.PL.M.AN | -í | | |
  | adverb (cell `ADV`) | -e/-ě | -y, -o (apart: `ADV -y`, `ADV -o`) | |

  Inflection shows the second ("small") palatalization (k→c, g/h→z,
  ch→š, r→ř; sk→šť, ck→čť before i/í); derivation the first ("big")
  one (k→č, g/h→ž, ch→š). Animacy-specific cells take rows whose
  animacy is known (a cs-only masculine without animacy enters only
  the pooled masculine cells), and the NOM.PL competitions leave out
  the animate masculines in -a (předseda, kolega, turista), whose
  -ové/-é is their paradigm's, not a choice against -i.
- **Thresholds (R11).** `dictionary` (every Wiktionary form), `ge1`
  (≥ 1 token in the corpus), `ge0.5pm`, `ge2pm` (≥ 0.5 and ≥ 2 per
  million). Dictionary forms are not attestation: Wiktionary's module
  generates biolozích whether or not anyone writes it. Each corpus
  threshold has a **strict** variant (`ge1_strict` …) that counts a
  form's tokens only when the form is neither another lexeme's
  (`form_ambiguous`: válku is válka's, not válek's) nor shared with a
  cell outside its copy group (LOC.SG parku is also GEN.SG and DAT.SG;
  VOC kluku also DAT/LOC.SG): rule, competition, spell-out, learner and
  R21 rows have both.
- **Frequency bases (R24).** `lemma` (tokens of the lemma form),
  `paradigm` (attested forms summed) and `paradigm_unambiguous` (the
  same without forms of other lexemes): homograph lemmas inflate the
  first two (jak 'yak' carries the conjunction's 33,277 tokens;
  `lemma_ambiguous` flags 4,794 in-domain noun lemmas).
- **Doublets (R19).** A root with both allomorphs in a cell takes its
  dominant variant (≥ 90% of the tokens, else the majority; a tie, such
  as two forms without tokens, counts against both rules), or counts
  as an exception (`doublet_exception`), or is left out
  (`doublet_excluded`); every table has all three codings.
- **Evidence sets (R15).** E0 nominal inflection (the root's noun and
  adjective cells); E1 adds -it/-et verbs with the changed stem
  (strašit); E2 adds every derivative with a changed stem.
- **Strata (R22).** inherited / borrowed whole / internal formation /
  unknown, from the English Wiktionary etymology templates; the NC
  sections add czetyl and the DeriNet Loanword flag (precedence
  Wiktionary > czetyl > DeriNet). The shared classifier makes "From
  Latin tragicus + Czech -ický" borrowed whole; the Czech plugin keeps
  that for an **adapted loan** (the Czech suffix is the equivalent of
  the foreign ending: -icus → -ický, -tiō → -ace, a foreign adjective →
  -ní; 182 lexemes) and makes a **hybrid**, a Czech formation on a
  borrowed stem (fan + -ouš + -ek → fanoušek, Welsh + -an → Velšan,
  mainstream + -ový; 38 lexemes), an internal formation
  (`stratum_wikt_basis` `adapted_loan`, `hybrid`). The sources disagree
  at times: en.wiktionary calls vzduch a borrowing from Russian and žák
  inherited, czetyl calls žák a Latin loan.

## Methods

### TP analyses (stage `tp`)

Every number is computed by the core (`lexpipe.tp`, `lexpipe.learners`);
`tpcells.py` only decides which roots and forms go into which row. The
full grid (target × trigger × subset × τ × evidence set × coding × rule)
is `analysis/czech/czech_tp_long.csv`; `czech_tp_summary.txt` has the
headline tables.

- **(a) Rule TP.** For each P cell: N = roots attested with the P
  allomorph, e = those whose P form lacks the change; a competing N
  allomorph shields the root. Non-trigger rows (-é, -em, -ech, the
  coronal and Vr vocative -e) put every such form in with no change:
  N = e by construction (R16).
- **(b) Competition TP.** Per cell and gender, slot A is the
  palatalizing (for non-targets the front) allomorph and slot B its
  competitor (LOC.SG -e vs -u; NOM.PL -i vs -ové/-é and -i vs -ové
  alone; LOC.PL -ích vs -ách/-ech for velars, -ech vs -ách for the
  rest; VOC -e vs -u; adverbs -e vs -y/-o, and -e vs -y and -e vs -o
  apart, since the -o adverbs blízko, daleko, těžko are a formation of
  their own). The `palatalize` row is the P-default rule (e = roots
  choosing B), `retain` the N-default rule (e = roots choosing A).
  Nested classes in Yang's order: the whole-word loans in
  -log/-urg/-graf (R27), the -ský/-cký adjectives (`sky`, R26: their
  NOM.PL -ští/-čtí and adverb -y are the suffix's), the other R26 suffix
  targets (-ák, -ík, -ník, -ek, -ka, -ička), monosyllables, loans,
  elsewhere; each class takes the rule with fewer exceptions. A class
  defined by the suffix comes before the etymological ones, so
  -ický adjectives never fall into `loan`.
- **(c) Spell-out TP, leave one cell out.** A root is
  `underspecified` for a tested cell when it shows the change at τ in
  its evidence set outside that cell (and its copies: VOC.PL is a copy
  of NOM.PL), else `prespecified`. The prediction is P for the first
  class (e = roots taking the N allomorph) and N for the second (e =
  roots taking P).
- **R17 rule family** (the cross-language comparison): R17a dorsal
  before front vowels (-e and -i), R17b dorsal before high front -i
  (with -e as its non-trigger cell), R17c r and t/d/n before -i, over
  every form whose ending starts with the vowel class (nouns of the
  adjectival declension, hajný and vysoká, are counted in the
  adjective rows); E2 adds derivatives, and for R17b the
  consonant-initial (kniž-ní, kníž-ka) and back-vowel suffixes
  (Praž-an), where the family predicts no change. The V sweep (R24)
  takes the V ∈ {500, 1000, 2000, 5000} most frequent noun roots of any
  final, on each frequency basis.
- **R22 derivation rows.** One row per derivational suffix for velar,
  r and c bases on the main derivative basis: P suffixes that
  palatalize the target (palatalize row, e = derivatives without the
  change), the non-trigger -ista and -ismus (N = e by design) and the
  shielding N suffixes.
- **Simulated learners (R18).** 1,000 vocabularies per V drawn by
  token frequency, at τ dictionary, ≥ 1 token and ≥ 1 token strict, on
  each of the three frequency bases; the share of learners for whom
  each rule is tolerated.

### Derivations (stage `derivations`)

Every in-domain noun (and the dual remnants) is a possible base. A
base–derivative pair is found by an English Wiktionary etymology
("kniha + -ní"), the base's headword line (adj=, dim=, f=), its
derived or related terms, a DeriNet 2.3 `Type=Derivation` edge (joined
on lemma, gender and animacy; Fictitious nodes dropped; the verbalizer
read from the last derivational morph, ConjugClass only as a check),
or a generated candidate (R21) in both spellings (vzdušný/vzduchný)
that is a Wiktionary headword or has corpus tokens of its own. The
derivative must decompose into (prefix +) base stem (plain or changed)
+ one of the listed suffixes:

| class | suffixes |
|---|---|
| P (palatalizing) | -it, -et, -ní/-ný, -ský/-cký, -ina, -í, -ek, -ka (-ička), -ík (-íček), -an, -ník, -nice |
| N (non-palatalizing) | -ovat, -ový/-ovní, -yně, -ův |
| NT (front vowel, no change) | -ista, -ismus (plain stem only: katolicismus is the learned -icismus, not katolík + -ismus) |
| competing pairs (R20, R23) | -ní/-ný vs -ový/-ovní; -it vs -ovat; -ka vs -yně |

Candidates are checked: the spelling must be the expected one (the
first palatalization for a P suffix; plain for N suffixes, whose
mutated spellings only find other words: pracovat ← práce, not prak),
the stem must not be shared by bases with other lemmas, the
candidate's own etymology must not name another base, an -ista/-ismus
candidate must not belong to an -ie noun (ekologista ← ekologie), and a
corpus-only candidate needs a stem of ≥ 4 letters, an attested lemma
form and ≥ 2 tokens of its own. Since the spelling check would make a P
suffix's outcome `pal` by construction, a **headword** candidate in the
plain spelling of a palatalizing suffix (pantofágní) is accepted and
flagged (`cand_headword_plain`, 24 pairs; 4 of the 5 on velar bases
are genuine exceptions, alkan is not). Which base a link belongs to:
derived and related terms belong to the entry that lists them, not to
every homograph of its lemma; a derivative found for several homograph
roots (Buddha/buddha, sok 'rival'/sok) is kept on one, a feminine
agent on the animate masculine; a related-terms link is dropped when
its derivative's own etymology names another base (vystrašit is vy- +
strašit), when the derivative is borrowed whole, or when it is an
inherited verb of an inherited noun (křik – křičet, lék – léčit: the
pair is related, its direction unknown; strach – strašný keeps its
link); a verb that is
the source of a deverbal base (obloha ← obložit, útok ← útočit, leh ←
lehat ~ ležet, tucha ← tušit) is no derivative of it; a derivative
that is a proper name (Sněžka) is dropped (`czech_derivations_report.txt`
counts each). The **basis** of a pair is its narrowest evidence:
`linked` (a Wiktionary link) ⊂ `wikt` (+ headword candidates; the main
basis) ⊂ `wikt+corpus` (+ corpus-only candidates) ⊂ `all` (+
DeriNet-only pairs; NC, never in a tracked table).

### Spell-out, no evidence, co-variation, -log table

- **Spell-out (R20).** Certain roots: hard feminine velars with an
  attested alternating DAT/LOC.SG (and ruka), or any velar noun with an
  attested alternating form (E0-certain). N = roots with an attested
  derivative from a competing pair, e = roots taking only the
  non-palatalizing member (doublets by coding); the opposite rule's e
  is reported too. Only real choices between the members count: -ka vs
  -yně only for animate masculine bases and feminine agents (a
  diminutive -ka of a feminine, bleška, dečka, is no -yně alternative,
  so the pair is empty for the feminine set), and -it vs -ovat without
  bases derived from a verb (záloha ← založit, sprcha ← spršet). Every
  row is split by the base's R26 suffix target (`base:root-final`:
  kniha, ruka, liga; `base:suffix`: the -ka of aktovka, bankovka): the
  root-final rows are the test of root specification. Per pair, τ,
  basis and stratum.
- **No evidence (R21).** The base class is read from the corpus at τ
  (and its strict variant): `alternating` (an attested P form with the
  change), `non-alternating` (only competing N allomorphs, or a P ending
  without the change), `no evidence`. Units are base × P suffix that
  palatalizes the base; outcome pal / plain / both. 3×2 tables per
  suffix, per base stratum, without the learned -ik(a)/-ikum → -ický
  units (kybernetika → kybernetický: possibly borrowed whole, or the
  suffix -ický), over derivatives that are internal formations only (an
  inherited derivative was formed in Proto-Slavic and is stored; a
  Czech coinage such as bioložka shows what the grammar does now: the
  decisive test), and the velar neuters apart. The counterexamples are
  listed by stratum at τ = dictionary; the lists at ≥ 1 token are
  filtered by per-word corpus attestation and go to
  `data/czech/czech_noevidence_counterexamples.txt` (untracked, R9).
- **Co-variation (R23).** Unit: noun root × competing pair (members as
  in R20). The base's inflectional class from LOC.SG (-e vs -u) or
  NOM.PL (-i vs -ové), coded `any` (any attested P form) or `dominant`;
  the derivative choice P / N / both (both left out, or counted as P,
  or as N). Per-pair odds ratios with Woolf intervals and Fisher's
  exact test, the Mantel–Haenszel OR over pair × base stratum, and a
  stratified permutation test (10,000 permutations within strata,
  one-sided). The logistic model every language fits
  (`lexpipe.stats.r23_line`: deriv_pal ~ base_class + log lemma
  frequency + base stratum + derivative stratum, base-clustered errors;
  needs the optional statsmodels) for the velar group.
- **-log table (R27).** Nouns in -log, -urg, -graf, -fil with the two
  classifiers every language shares (`lexpipe.suffixoids`): learned
  suffixoid or lookalike (R26 etymology/category evidence, the combining
  -o- of cs.wiktionary lexemes, the English gloss's reflex, -olog
  professions; profil is a lookalike) and person / thing from the first
  gloss: English glosses without their parenthesised explanation (the
  agent-noun rule read paragraf's 'section (part of a legal
  document …)' as -ent), Czech glosses with the Czech rules only (the
  English -or rule read dekalog's 'soubor …'); curated decisions in
  `curated/czech/suffixoid_classes.csv` (dekalog and paragraf are
  things). Their NOM.PL, LOC.SG and LOC.PL allomorphs per τ; aggregates
  over the learned suffixoid nouns.

## Results (full data, 2026-10-23, after the audit fixes)

### Inflection: the rule is exceptionless, the variation is in allomorph choice

**Rule TP (a).** Wherever a palatalizing allomorph is attested, the
stem changes. At ≥ 1 token (and at ≥ 1 token strict) every velar and r
trigger row has e = 0:

| target | cell | N (≥ 1 token) | e | θ | N strict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| velar | NOM.PL m.an -i | 442 | 0 | 72.6 | 427 |
| velar | DAT/LOC.SG f -e | 1,124 | 0 | 160.0 | 1,000 |
| velar | LOC.PL m -ích | 484 | 0 | 78.3 | 393 |
| velar | adjective NOM.PL.M.AN -í | 456 | 0 | 74.5 | 444 |
| r | NOM.PL m.an -i | 184 | 0 | 35.3 | 178 |
| r | DAT/LOC.SG f -e | 131 | 0 | 26.9 | 114 |

At the dictionary level the only exceptions are cs.wiktionary table
forms that the data layer labels `unexplained` and that have no tokens
(epistemologe, metodickí, psychiatre): velar NOM.PL e = 2 of 1,272,
adjectives e = 12 of 2,813, and the velar vocative row fails (e = 8 of
13) on such forms alone. The non-trigger rows behave as designed
(INS.SG -em: N = e = 1,248 velar roots at ≥ 1 token).

of forms that are neither another lexeme's nor shared with another
cell) leaves LOC.SG and VOC almost empty, because their -u is always
also GEN.SG or DAT/LOC.SG (velar LOC.SG m.in N = 4, VOC N = 2; t/d/n
LOC.SG 56, baseline 15): the ≥ 1-token -u rows of these two cells, and
the learner shares built on them, are upper bounds. NOM.PL and LOC.PL
keep their verdicts strictly (velar NOM.PL N = 514: 95 / 422; LOC.PL
m.in N = 308: 8 / 300).

Read across: velars avoid the palatalizing allomorph where a
competitor exists in the LOC.SG of neuters (123 of 126 velar neuters
take -u, against 82 of 134 baseline neuters), in the vocative (-u; the
baseline takes -e) and in the LOC.PL of neuters (-ách). They do
**not** avoid it in NOM.PL and the masculine LOC.PL: four fifths of the
velar animates take -i (kluci, žáci) while three quarters of the
baseline animates take -ové/-é, and -ích is the norm. Nor in
**adverbs**: the earlier reading "velars avoid -e in adverbs (hezky)"
was a suffix confound. The -y adverb is the -ský/-cký suffix's (česky,
typicky: 155 of 156 such adjectives at ≥ 1 token); the other velar
adjectives form their adverb with -e when the competitor is -y (lehce,
krátce, hluboce, draze: 38 of 39 at ≥ 1 token, θ = 10.6; 108 of 111 in
the dictionary), and the -o adverbs (blízko, daleko, těžko) are a
formation of their own with no tolerated rule (-e vs -o: 40 roots, 11
/ 30). LOC.SG -u is the type-level default of every hard inanimate
masculine (velar 99.5%, baseline 97%, t/d/n 97% take -u), so it is no
velar-specific avoidance at this level, whatever its token-level
conditioning (IJP 223; Bermel & Knittl). The same holds at the
dictionary level (velar LOC.SG m.in 2,039 of 2,045 roots -u; baseline
2,210 of 2,237). At ≥ 1 token the doublet codings (R19) change no
velar verdict; at the dictionary level, where 355 velar inanimates list
both -ích and -ách/-ech and 296 of them are true doublets, leaving the
doublets out makes "LOC.PL m.in takes -ích" tolerated (e = 58 of
1,774).

**Nested classes**, velar NOM.PL, ≥ 1 token: -log/-urg/-graf take -ové
without exception (retain N = 44, e = 0); the other suffix targets
(-ák, -ík, -ník, -ek) palatalize (N = 283, e = 20, θ = 50.1); loans
palatalize (N = 16, e = 4, θ = 5.8); elsewhere palatalize is
tolerated (N = 150, e = 20, θ = 29.9); monosyllables (25 -i, 12 -ové;
θ = 10.2) have no tolerated rule. In LOC.SG m.in, VOC and LOC.PL every
class takes the same rule. Adverbs: the -ský/-cký class takes -y
(retain N = 156, e = 1); the remaining velar adjectives take -e against
-y (N = 39, e = 1) and have no rule against -o (N = 40, e = 11 / 30).
Before the audit, R26 suffix targets needed an etymology template or
category: most -ský/-cký adjectives fell into `loan` or `elsewhere`,
which made `elsewhere` fail as retain (N = 123, e = 28).

**Simulated learners** (V = 1,000, ≥ 1 token, lemma basis): 99.9% of
the learners end with "velar NOM.PL takes -i", 98.8% with "baseline
NOM.PL takes -ové/-é", 100% with "LOC.SG m.in takes -u" (velar and
baseline; an upper bound, see strict counting) and "LOC.PL m.in takes
-ích/-ech". At V = 5,000 the velar NOM.PL rule holds for 91.5% of the

the particle and the preposition); at ≥ 1 token strict, 96.0%, 99.1%
and 99.7%. V = 1,000 results do not depend on the basis.

## Research notes

**Two palatalizations, suffix by suffix.** Inflection shows the
second palatalization before LOC/DAT -e, NOM.PL -i, LOC.PL -ích, the
adjectival NOM.PL -í and adverbial -e; derivation the first, before
-ek, -ka, -ný/-ní, -it, -ina; -ský gives k → c (otrocký) beside h → ž
and ch → š (IJP 226 and 228 for inflection, 730 for derivation).
Cavirani & Vanden Wyngaerd (2026)
analyse three suffix-specific patterns triggered by floating melodic
features rather than by the suffix vowel, and Poĺomská (2025) types
the suffixes by the palatalization they trigger (-yně none; -ek, -ice
and -ě/-e three different types; -ka and -an palatalize without a
front vowel; r in a branching onset patterns apart: vnitro → vnitřek
but večer → večírek). The R17 rows say the same in TP terms: a
rule stated on the following vowel fails (front -em and -ech do not
trigger, consonant-initial -ka and -ní do), while the suffix rows of
R22 are each near-exceptionless. For generative treatments of Slavic
palatalization see Rubach (1993, on Slovak; 2011), and for Czech
Ketner (2005), on featural morphemes.

**Allomorph choice is where resistance lives.** IJP 223 says velar,
r, d, t, n stems mostly take LOC.SG -u "because -ě(e) requires an
alternation", while noting that the distribution is not fixed
(syntactic function, region, loans and abstracts); Bermel & Knittl
(2012a, 2012b), Bermel, Knittl & Russell (2015) and Bermel & Knittl
(2023) study the -u/-e competition (with GEN.SG -a/-u) against corpus
frequency and speakers' judgments, and
Štícha (2009) contrasts usage with codification (ve vlaku vs v
potoce). In our counts the type-level -u default is shared by every
hard inanimate masculine, so the avoidance shows up in the neuters
(IJP 260: -ko/-go/-ho/-cho neuters take -u) and the vocative (IJP 225:
k/g/h/ch stems take -u, bůh and člověk aside), not in LOC.SG
masculines at the type level, nor in the adverbs once the -ský/-cký
suffix, whose adverb is -y, is set apart. For NOM.PL, IJP 226 gives the
-ové classes (monosyllables, -log/-urg/-graf/-fil/-fob/-nom loans, the
předseda type, surnames) and -é for -tel, -an, -ista and -at/-it loans
(diplomaté), and Rusínová (1987) studies the -i/-ové distribution; our
nested rows confirm -log/-urg/-graf (44 of 44 -ové) but find the velar
monosyllables split (25 -i, 12 -ové). In the feminine DAT/LOC.SG the
change is obligatory even in loans and names (IJP 370: Malaga →
Malaze, Mekka → Mekce).

**Resistance is cell-specific.** Nouns that refuse NOM.PL -i still
palatalize in LOC.PL -ích and in their -ka feminine (biologové but
biolozích, bioložka; mágové, mázích: MorfFlex's basic forms, IJP 226,
228). A learner who sees these stems alternate would leave them
underspecified; their -ové is a lexical property of the allomorph
choice, not of a fixed stem.

**What the numbers say about the two accounts.** (1) Derivational
palatalization does not depend on attested inflectional alternation:
bases without evidence at any threshold palatalize in 95–99% of their
P-suffix derivatives, as alternating bases do, inherited ones
included, with strict counting too, and in 94–98% of their derivatives
that are Czech internal formations (R21). Strict inflection dependence
predicts the opposite for these bases. (2) Allomorph selection is not predicted by alternation
elsewhere in the paradigm (spell-out LOO holds in one direction per
cell), and derivative suffix choice does not co-vary with inflectional
class beyond the baseline (R23). (3) What the learner can extract is a
set of suffix- and cell-specific rules, each tolerated: palatalize
before every P suffix and P inflectional allomorph; choose -u in
LOC.SG masculines, -ové for -log nouns, -i elsewhere in velar NOM.PL,
-y in the adverbs of -ský/-cký adjectives and -e in the others. (4)
Where the spell-out account makes its sharpest prediction, roots that
certainly alternate, the derivative choice is split by morphology, not
uniform: a velar of the suffix -ka takes -ový/-ovní without exception
(148 of 148 feminine roots), root-final velars divide between -ní/-ný
and -ový/-ovní (24 vs 35 at ≥ 1 token, neither rule tolerated), and
-it vs -ovat follows the stratum (inherited bases -it, borrowed ones
-ovat). Neither "certain roots choose the palatalizing member" nor the
earlier "-ový/-ovní is the choice of 265 of 294 certain roots" holds
once the suffix -ka is told from root-final k; the feminizing -ka is
chosen over -yně by animate masculines (N = 250, e = 19).

**Phonetics.** Czech palatal stops have an affricated release, ř is a
trilled fricative obstruent, and ti/di/ni are hard in most loans
(Šimáčková, Podlipský & Chládková 2012; IJP 914, 126), which is why
t/d/n are inferred rows here and why the coronal E2 row has
exceptions.

## Open questions

- Cell attribution of syncretic tokens (LOC.SG -u, VOC -u) needs a
  tagged corpus (MorphoDiTa/UDPipe on Leipzig, or KonText); until then
  the token-level LOC.SG figures are upper bounds for -u.
- Token-level conditioning of LOC.SG (preposition v/na vs o, region,
  genre: IJP 223; Bermel & Knittl) is not modelled.
- The R23 logistic model is fitted when statsmodels is installed; a
  mixed model with base and suffix intercepts (R lme4) was not
  attempted.
- Headword candidates are 89% precise in the hand check; a gloss or
  semantic filter (or DeriNet agreement) could raise that, at a cost
  in recall.
- czetyl and en.wiktionary disagree on some strata (žák, politika,
  vzduch); a hand-adjudicated stratum list for the counterexamples
  would settle the inherited cases.
- The -ický adjectives: whether kybernetický is kybernetik(a) + -ský or
  a borrowed whole is not decided here; the R21 tables give both.
- Velar monosyllables split between -i and -ové in NOM.PL; whether
  frequency, animacy or ethnonym status decides is open.
- The -ka suffix's categorical -ový/-ovní (aktovka → aktovkový) against
  the split root-final velars: a property of the suffix -ka (its -k- is
  never followed by a P suffix), or of the root's specification? A
  test needs -ka words with an attested -ní/-ný derivative, of which
  there are none.
- The R26 list leaves out the neuter diminutives in -ko (-íčko,
  -ečko: jablíčko); they are no suffix targets here.
- The per-word lists of tracked reports are printed at τ = dictionary
  (R9); whether word lists filtered by Leipzig attestation may be
  tracked is for the authors to decide (`DATA_LICENSE.md`). The
  manual worklist is still chosen by comparison with MorfFlex (§11 of
  the design, pending the authors).
- Homograph lemmas are flagged, not split: a tagged corpus would give
  each homograph its own tokens.
